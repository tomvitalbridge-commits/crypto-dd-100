**The case for**

1. Founder track record (3/4) — CEO Rand Hindi founded Snips, which was acquired; CTO Pascal Paillier co-invented the Paillier scheme. Zama itself has shipped FHE libraries since 2020, but no crypto product at scale yet.
2. Token utility & value accrual (3/4) — Protocol fees are paid in ZAMA and burned, and operators must stake ZAMA. The burn mechanism is live, but fee volume at listing is unknown and probably small next to 5% emissions.
3. Technical leadership (4/4) — CTO is a recognised cryptographer; the in-house team maintains the TFHE-rs library and the fhEVM stack with published specs and a patent portfolio.

**The case against**

1. Mint / freeze / blacklist powers (1/4) — mint() has no cap and is gated only by a role the DAO admin grants; emissions of 5% of supply a year are by design. There is no transfer freeze or blacklist.
2. Governance design & decentralisation path (1/4) — At listing an Aragon DAO acting through a 3/5 multisig with unnamed signers holds the token and bridge admin roles; operator-majority governance is announced, and token holders have no vote. No dated decentralisation milestones were found.
3. Problem clarity & real demand (2/4) — On-chain confidentiality is a real gap, but at listing the usage evidence was a confidential USDT transfer, one payroll pilot (Bron) and a token auction. That shows demand for the token, not for paid protocol usage.
