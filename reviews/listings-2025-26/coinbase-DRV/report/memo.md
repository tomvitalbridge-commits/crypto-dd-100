**The case for**

1. Product maturity (4/4) — Options protocol live since 2021; the V2 exchange was audited from 2023 to Mar 2025 and was in production well over 12 months before listing.
2. Founder track record (3/4) — Founders come from equity options and market making and have run Lyra/Derive since 2021 through two product generations; Derive now earns ~$4.1M annualised revenue, a modest scale.
3. Problem clarity & real demand (3/4) — Self-custodial options and perps with paying users: revenue doubled to $1.03M in Feb–Apr 2026 and OI passed $1B in March 2026, though the OI figure is self-reported.

**The case against**

1. Mint / freeze / blacklist powers (1/4) — Despite a 'fixed supply, no new tokens' policy, supply was raised 50% by deploying a new OFT on HyperEVM and linking it through LayerZero; nothing technical stops the Foundation from repeating this after an off-chain vote.
2. Securities-law risk (1/4) — Token value is tied to revenue-funded buybacks while the Foundation controls supply and treasury and issued 500M tokens to fund growth.
3. Dependency risk (1/4) — Prices come from the team's own signed feeds (2 required signers in the mainnet config), the chain runs on a semi-permissioned OP Stack sequencer, collateral arrives over bridges from four chains, and DRV supply spans chains via LayerZero.
4. Claim contradicted: Help centre: total supply is fixed at 1,000,000,000 DRV and 'no new tokens will be minted' vs observed 1,500,000,000 (33% gap)
